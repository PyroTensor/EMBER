# Spherical Gaseous Diffusion Flames, Soot and Extinction Limits

> PROXY DOCUMENT - written by the EMBER team to resemble NASA PSI / NTRS reporting on the Advanced
> Combustion via Microgravity Experiments (ACME) suite in the ISS Combustion Integrated Rack. ACME
> burned gaseous fuels; EMBER's proxy dataset represents these as pre-vaporised heptane / methanol
> burner flames. Numbers are illustrative.

## Background

ACME studied laminar, non-premixed gaseous flames from small spherical porous burners and coflow
burners, without buoyancy. In 0g the flame around a spherical burner is a nearly perfect sphere. Its
diameter grows slowly over tens of seconds, because the only process removing products is diffusion.

## Key findings

1. **Larger, dimmer, longer-lasting flames.** Microgravity diffusion flames were larger than the
   same flames on Earth. Residence times were long, temperatures lower, and colours bluer when soot
   was limited.
2. **Soot and radiation.** When soot formed, it radiated strongly from the large flame volume. The
   flame then lost heat through radiation as it grew, and could reach radiative extinction. Peak
   radiometer fluxes of about 2-4 kW/m^2 were recorded at 21-30 % O2 in the EMBER proxy runs.
3. **Low-flow extinction.** With very low or no imposed flow, flames at 18 % O2 and reduced pressure
   often extinguished. A small coflow of about 2 cm/s widened the flammable range.

<!-- page: 2 -->
## Pressure and diluent effects

Lowering the pressure from 101.3 to 60 kPa made flames larger and reduced soot. Overall
flammability decreased slightly, as LOI rose. Argon diluent, which has a lower heat capacity than N2,
made flames hotter and more robust. CO2 diluent suppressed flames.

## Implications for spacecraft fire safety

- Smoke detectors tuned to soot particles from 1g fires may respond differently to the low-soot, blue
  flames of microgravity, which give off more ultrafine particles.
- Radiation from large, slow-growing 0g flames can heat nearby surfaces over long periods, so a
  fire that is not producing visible flames can still damage surrounding materials.
