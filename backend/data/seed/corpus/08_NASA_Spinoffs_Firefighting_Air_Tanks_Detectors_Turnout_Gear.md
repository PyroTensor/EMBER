# NASA Spinoffs for Terrestrial Firefighting and Fire Detection

> Extracted from NASA Space Apps 2026 *Flame in Freefall* resources: *Fireman's Air Tanks*
> (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9582`, NTRS `20030005480`, 1976), *Fire Detector* (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9407`,
> NTRS `20070018916`, 1978), and *New Technology for the Fire Attire* (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9918`, NTRS
> `20030002834`, 1982).

## 1. Fireman's Air Tanks (Composite Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus - SCBA, 1976)

- **Space heritage.** Conventional steel SCBA breathing cylinders weighed over **33 pounds (15 kg)**,
  restricting firefighter mobility, agility, and endurance. Through a joint development program between
  **NASA Johnson Space Center (JSC)** and **Scott Aviation** (a division of A-T-O Inc.), composite pressure
  vessel technology originally engineered for rocket motor casings and Apollo lunar portable life-support
  systems was adapted for municipal firefighting.
- **Engineering and terrestrial impact.** By replacing heavy steel tanks with a seamless **aluminum liner
  overwrapped with resin-impregnated, filament-wound glass fibers** pressurized to **4,500 psi** (double the
  pressure of conventional tanks), the 30-minute breathing system weight dropped from 33 pounds to **20 pounds
  (~40 % lighter)**. The NASA-Scott program also introduced a wide-vision face mask, a frame-mounted shoulder
  harness that shifted weight to the hips, and a personalized low-pressure audible alarm so firefighters in
  smoke-filled structures could distinguish their own low-air warning from a teammate's.

## 2. Fire Detector: Smoke vs. Dust Discrimination (Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector, 1978)

- **Space heritage.** Skylab and Space Shuttle Orbiters required incipient fire detectors capable of sensing
  early sub-micron pyrolysis aerosols and ionized combustion products in microgravity—where non-buoyant smoke
  travels slowly along cabin HVAC streamlines—prior to open flame or visible smoke, without triggering false
  alarms from floating cabin dust. This lineage directly preceded ISS aerosol characterization flights
  (**SAME**, **SAME-R**, and **DAFT**).
- **Engineering and terrestrial impact.** Derived from the Space Shuttle orbiter detector and commercialized
  as the **Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector**, the unit actively samples air and uses **particle-size
  discrimination circuitry** to separate sub-micron combustion particulates from larger ambient dust particles.
  Deployed across industrial facilities, commercial buildings, and high-dust underground mining operations, it
  eliminates nuisance dust false alarms while detecting incipient overheating before visible flames appear.

## 3. New Technology for the Fire Attire (Project FIRES, 1982)

- **Space heritage.** Following the **Apollo 1** cabin fire, NASA developed non-flammable spacesuit and cabin
  textiles—including **polyimide**, **PBI (polybenzimidazole)**, **Nomex**, Beta cloth, and aluminized
  radiant-heat barriers—screened under **NASA-STD-6001B** and tested in microgravity fabric investigations
  (**BASS**, **BASS-II**, **BASS-M**, and **SoFIE**).
- **Engineering and terrestrial impact.** Under **Project FIRES** (Firefighters Integrated Response Equipment
  System), jointly sponsored by **NASA and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA) U.S. Fire
  Administration** and managed technically by **NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC)** with prime
  contractor **Grumman**, NASA space-suit materials were integrated into a complete structural firefighting
  ensemble (coats, pants, boots, helmets, and gloves)—substantially reducing garment weight while improving
  flame resistance and radiant-heat protection.
