# NASA Spinoffs for Terrestrial Firefighting and Fire Detection

> Compiled from NASA Space Apps 2026 *Flame in Freefall* resources: *Fireman's Air Tanks*
> (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9582`, 1976), *Fire Detector* (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9407`, 1978), and *New
> Technology for the Fire Attire* (`spinoff.nasa.gov/node/9918`, 1982).

## 1. Fireman's Air Tanks (Composite Self-Contained Breathing Apparatus - SCBA, 1976)

- **Space heritage.** Traditional steel SCBA cylinders weighed roughly 33 pounds (15 kg), limiting
  firefighter mobility and endurance. Together with **NASA Johnson Space Center**, A-T-O Inc.'s **Scott
  Aviation** adapted filament-wound **composite overwrapped pressure vessel (COPV)** technology—originally
  developed for Apollo lunar life-support backpacks and rocket motor casings—into aluminum-lined,
  filament-wrapped high-pressure breathing cylinders.
- **Terrestrial impact.** The resulting 30-minute backpack system weighed only **20 pounds—13 pounds
  (~40 %) lighter** than conventional steel firefighting tanks—directly lowering physiological heat
  strain and extending safe working duration inside structural and industrial fires.

## 2. Fire Detector: Smoke vs. Dust Discrimination (Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector, 1978)

- **Space heritage.** Skylab and Space Shuttle orbiters required incipient fire detectors capable of
  sensing early sub-micron pyrolysis aerosols in microgravity—where non-buoyant smoke travels slowly
  along cabin HVAC streamlines—without triggering nuisance alarms from floating cabin dust, lint, or
  humidity. This work informed subsequent ISS aerosol characterization experiments (**SAME**, **SAME-R**,
  **DAFT**, **DAFT-2**).
- **Terrestrial impact.** Commercialized as the **Brunswick Incipient Fire Detector**, the sensor
  actively samples air and discriminates between sub-micron combustion particles and larger airborne dust
  particles. Deployed in mining operations, industrial facilities, and aircraft cargo bays, it
  substantially reduces false alarms while preserving rapid response to smouldering and low-soot fires.

## 3. New Technology for the Fire Attire (Project FIRES, 1982)

- **Space heritage.** Following the **Apollo 1** cabin fire, NASA developed non-flammable spacesuit and
  crew garment materials including **PBI (polybenzimidazole)**, **Nomex**, Beta cloth, and aluminized
  radiant-heat barriers evaluated under NASA flammability standards (**NASA-STD-6001B** and microgravity
  fabric tests in **BASS-II** and **SoFIE**).
- **Terrestrial impact.** Through **Project FIRES** (Firefighters Integrated Response Equipment System),
  jointly sponsored by **NASA and the Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)**, space-grade thermal
  textiles (Nomex/PBI outer shells, moisture barriers, and lightweight thermal liners) were integrated
  into modern structural firefighter turnout gear—cutting ensemble weight while improving flashover and
  radiant-heat survival.
